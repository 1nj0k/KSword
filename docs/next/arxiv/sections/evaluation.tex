Insertion's driver-body median is 2595.3\,\us{} (maximum 3053.9\,\us{}), with a
rendezvous median of 111.6\,\us{} (maximum 148.2\,\us{}). Removal's corresponding
medians are 1107.4 and 55.8\,\us{}, with maxima of 1275.2 and 393.8\,\us{}.
Process creation and command work expand the application-level control window
to roughly 20\,ms. These boundaries answer different questions and cannot be
substituted for one another.

The largest measured command-overlap gap is 6.2300\,ms during insertion. Sham
conditions also contain multi-millisecond gaps; the on-mode sham has a
16.1823\,ms RTT outlier outside its command window. We therefore do not call
6.23\,ms the exact transition pause or subtract a sham maximum to produce a
causal pause estimate. The closed-loop client limits offered load, and scheduler,
socket, pacing, and observation work all contribute to the recorded intervals.

The historical cohort supplies more detailed phase instrumentation across ten
Windows-only insert/remove pairs. Its insertion-body median is 2.8005\,ms,
resource preparation 2.65375\,ms, and rendezvous envelope 149.5\,\us{}. Across
forty CPU-entry records, median state-capture/control-selection time is
13.85\,\us{}, VMCS programming 5.0\,\us{}, and entry continuation 24.1\,\us{}.
Entry-return skew has a 4.6\,\us{} median and 28.3\,\us{} maximum. A single cold
EPT-build observation is 6.0918\,ms in preparation. Parallel intervals overlap
and must not be added to estimate total pause. These older measurements
demonstrate bounded observed skew, not simultaneous entry or a current-build
replication of every phase statistic.

The historical version has twenty complete cycles: ten idle and ten with both

A write-isolation trial observes an A5-filled original page, a D1-filled private

Sparse CPUID snapshots reduce the median from 2944.212 to 2915.062\,ns relative
to the matching nested full-snapshot reference, an observed 0.99\% change with
overlapping ranges. RTT does not improve. The sparse nested mode still costs
8.7594 times the off-mode CPUID time and 30.6914\% more loopback RTT. A small
telemetry optimization therefore does not resolve the major remaining cost.

Adding 512 VMREADs to ordinary residency increases median CPUID latency from
2905.918 to 6875.552\,ns. Dividing that difference by 512 estimates 7.753\,ns
per added read, including loop overhead. The 256-read result is consistent with
a similar marginal scale. Removing two reads cannot account for a
microsecond-scale end-to-end gap.

A separate million-exit CPU~0 profile measures an average 382 TSC cycles for
the C dispatcher, including 97 cycles for initial VMCS reads; an empty
timestamp pair costs 25 cycles. An invariant-TSC calibration near 2.112\,GHz
converts 382 cycles to approximately 181\,ns. This profile averages a mixture
of exit types and excludes entry/exit assembly and outer-hypervisor work. It
cannot be directly subtracted from a CPUID-only measurement to isolate Hyper-V
cost. It identifies unmeasured paths for the next attribution step. RTT windows
are dominated by HLT, MSR, and hypercall exits, so reducing CPUID work alone is
not a demonstrated networking optimization.

\paragraph{Exit and memory accounting.}
A historical nested benchmark window records 19,489,348 resident dispatches,
approximately 167,733 per second. VMREAD, HLT, VMWRITE, EPT violation, and CPUID
account for approximately 6.36, 5.64, 2.65, 2.55, and 0.82 million events.
The INVEPT-wrapper counter records 101,233 calls, about 874.4 per second, with
zero failures. Counter queries have different interval boundaries; their
denominators must not be mixed. These windows include startup and controller
work and are not isolated steady-state rates or global outer-Hyper-V counts.
An intercepted INVVPID instruction category is available, but an actual
hardware INVVPID total is not. Actual newly ORed A/D bits are zero in this
window, so it does not establish all A/D writeback semantics at runtime.

The historical optimized structures reserve a computed 3.25\,MiB of relevant
shadow tables, snapshots, and A/D ledger capacity per active CPU, or 13\,MiB
for four CPUs. This is a structure-capacity calculation, not a measured total
driver footprint. The increase trades memory and preparation cost for fewer
shadow-cache capacity failures.

\subsection{Reversible HTTP policy corruption}
\label{sec:application}
The application fixture uses an unmodified BusyBox HTTP server to serve a
4096-byte file-cache page containing a price policy. A separate fixed-schema
consumer validates the complete body and emits \code{quote-1250} for the valid
policy or \code{reject-corrupt-policy} for the zeroed replacement. HTTP status
200 alone is insufficient. A privileged guest helper uses mlock/pagemap to pin
and identify the page; this is explicit instrumentation.

The EPT intervention targets GPA \code{0x02479000}, with original Windows~1
backing \code{0xCCF89000} and replacement \code{0x204FD4000}. In three complete
trials, the observed decision sequence is valid quote, rejection, valid quote.
The strict HTTP/PFN join validates ninety responses, ten per stage per trial.
A second analysis tolerating unrelated serial-line interleaving validates
ninety-one policy observations; the extra observation is not counted by the
strict contiguous join. Table~\ref{tab:application-runs} retains the fourth,
incomplete attempt, whose bounded observer expires after five mapped samples.
Its successful removal and cleanup are retained. Extending the observation
window precedes the final complete trial.

Windows boot, VMware PID/creation time, TinyCore boot ID, and server
PID/creation ticks remain unchanged within the respective intervals. Each
trial has advancing, fresh PFN samples. Post-run source review, however, found
that the measured policy collector printed its holder-alive field as a constant
one. The analysis explicitly reports \code{independentHolderLiveness=false};
that field is not evidence of liveness at each response. Fresh PFN observations
and server identities remain usable, with this limitation. The collector was
subsequently changed to perform \code{kill -0}, but that change was not rerun.
A read-only pre-comparator collection stall and an old-holder create-new
rejection are retained as setup failures, not application successes.

The useful capability is reversible data-fault injection from containing
Windows without extending VMware and without overwriting the source data page.
Direct writing also works and may be the simpler choice when guest write
authority and restoration bytes are available. The fixture does not establish
production fault tolerance, a general JSON oracle, or agent-free page discovery.

\subsection{Intermediate Hyper-V: a negative compatibility result}
\label{sec:hyperv}
After upgrading Windows~1 to Pro, installing its Hyper-V role, and rebooting
outside measured intervals, a Generation~1 two-vCPU TinyCore fixture boots
through normal \code{/init}. The first two rebuilt ISO images fail in ISOLINUX;
the third succeeds. These setup failures are retained, with no claim that one
specific image field has been isolated as their cause.

In this configuration, Windows~1 is an \emph{inner Hyper-V root partition}.
Its CPUID.1 ECX is \code{0xFEFA3203}, with VMX bit~5 clear. The \ks{} capability
query returns \code{STATUS\_NOT\_SUPPORTED} (\code{0xC00000BB}), and both prepare
and resident-start requests are refused before a control IOCTL is submitted.
No resident processors or prepared resources are reported. A first CLI version
returned empty output with exit code~2; the corrected shared engine reports
the query stage and \code{controlSubmitted=false}. Neither is successful insertion.

Windows boot identity and child VM GUID remain stable around the first
admission attempt. The worker remains PID~2824 with the same creation time,
and child uptime advances from 203.659 to 203.784 seconds. The guest has its own
recorded boot ID and repeated normal-init heartbeats. This is positive evidence
for the Hyper-V/TinyCore fixture and negative evidence for \ks{} admission at
that insertion point.

Microsoft describes Hyper-V as a hypervisor below its root partition, whose
management stack creates child partitions~\cite{hyperv-architecture}. Exposing
nested extensions to a managed child~\cite{hyperv-nested} does not establish
VMX execution authority for another monitor in the inner root partition.
Installing the role therefore changes the topology; it does not simply replace
VMware with another process inside an otherwise unchanged Windows kernel.
We make no impossibility claim about all alternative architectures or future
builds. Descendant EPT control and live Hyper-V interposition remain unsupported
by these measurements.
